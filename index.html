The Wanamaker Quiz

A hundred years ago John Wanamaker said half his advertising was wasted but he didn't know which half. The five numbers below tell you whether anything has changed.

Prof. Dr. Markus Rach

Question 1 of 5 . Marketing accountability

What percentage of senior marketing leaders can prove marketing's value to the business?

50%
Your guess
50%
Actual
52%

Gartner 2024 Marketing Analytics Survey, polling 479 senior marketing leaders. Just over half of the people running the marketing function can demonstrate, with evidence, that marketing produces business value. The other 48 percent cannot. A hundred years after Wanamaker, the profession is two percentage points beyond his benchmark. If you cannot prove your value, eventually you get fired. That is the lesson of Question 2.

Source: Gartner, Marketing Analytics Survey 2024.

Spencer Stuart's CMO Tenure Study, 2024 edition, Fortune 500 companies. Four point three years. The shortest tenure in the C suite, except for the COO. CEOs stay 7.6 years. CFOs stay 4.7. CMOs are replaced fastest because their contribution to the business is the hardest to prove. The S&P 500 number for 2025 dropped further, to 4.1 years.

Source: Spencer Stuart, CMO Tenure Study 2024 and 2025.

Question 3 of 5 . The role itself

What percentage of Fortune 500 companies still have a Chief Marketing Officer role at all?

85%
Your guess
85%
Actual
66%

Only two thirds of Fortune 500 companies had a C suite marketing leader in 2024. Down 8 percentage points from the year before. Companies are not just replacing CMOs faster, they are eliminating the role entirely. Marketing responsibility gets absorbed into the Chief Growth Officer, the Chief Customer Officer, the Chief Revenue Officer, or split across product and sales. If you cannot prove value, you are not just replaceable. You are deletable.

Source: Spencer Stuart, 2024 Fortune 500 CMO research.

Question 4 of 5 . The skill gap

30%
Your guess
30%
Actual

Marketing Week survey, over 3,000 brand side marketers. Data and analytics is the single biggest skill gap they report, for the second year running. Above creative skills. Above brand strategy. Above social media. The profession knows it has the wrong capabilities for the problems it now has to solve. Yet, as Question 5 shows, the people most in need of upskilling are the ones least likely to get it.

Source: Marketing Week Career and Salary Survey 2024.

Question 5 of 5 . The upskilling trap

What percentage of CMOs and marketing directors say their employer does NOT give them the chance to upskill?

35%

35%

Sixty one percent. The senior level is the least trained level. The CMOs whose tenure is shortest, whose role is being eliminated fastest, whose skill gap is the largest, are also the executives least likely to be given the time and budget to fix any of it. Compare this to middle management at 50 percent and junior marketers at 39 percent. The skill gap widens upward, exactly where the accountability question is asked.

Source: Marketing Week Career and Salary Survey 2024.
